Part 1Why cut a network into pieces?

One flat network, one big room

Picture a school where every device, 600 student laptops, the staff PCs, the finance server, the CCTV cameras and the visitors' phones, all sit on one network. Three things go wrong:

  • Performance. A flat network is one broadcast domain: the group of devices that receive a broadcast. When a device sends a message to everyone (a DHCP Discover, "I need an IP address", or an ARP request, "who has 10.0.0.5?"), all 700 devices receive it and must process it, even though it is irrelevant to almost all of them. With hundreds of devices doing this, the network fills with traffic nobody needs.
  • Security. Any device can reach any other. A visitor's infected laptop can reach the finance server, and malware can move from device to device across the whole network (lateral movement). One breach spreads everywhere.
  • Management. You cannot give the cameras different rules from the laptops, one problem affects everyone, and finding a faulty device means searching the whole building.

Segmentation

Network segmentation is dividing one network into smaller, separate parts called segments, so that traffic stays inside its own segment and anything that crosses between segments passes through a boundary device (a router, Layer 3 switch or firewall) where it can be checked or blocked.

One division gives two things: smaller segments carry less broadcast traffic, so congestion falls, and segments are walled off from each other, so a breach in one cannot reach the rest. Segmentation is not only a security measure.

Part 2Subnetting: the idea

Card A · An IP address has a network part and a host part

Every device on a network has an IP address, written as four numbers from 0 to 255, for example 10.20.30.15. The address has two parts, like a street name and a house number:

  • the network part (here 10.20.30) says which network, or subnet, the device is on;
  • the host part (here .15) says which device it is inside that subnet.

A subnet is one range of addresses that share the same network part: 10.20.30.1 to 10.20.30.254 is one subnet, 10.20.40.1 to 10.20.40.254 is another. A setting called the subnet mask tells each device where the network part ends. You need to know it exists; you do not calculate it.

Card B · What subnetting does

  • Devices in the same subnet talk directly, through the switch.
  • To reach a different subnet, traffic must go to a router (or a Layer 3 switch). The router forwards it only towards the right subnet, and it can filter it on the way: this is where firewall rules sit.
  • A router does not forward broadcasts. So a broadcast stays inside its own subnet: each subnet is its own broadcast domain.
  • Devices with similar needs (all the servers, all the cameras) are put in the same subnet, which keeps the address space organized and lets the technician write one rule for the whole group.

Choosing a size: a subnet must be big enough for every device in the group, with room to grow, but not so big that most of its addresses are wasted.

Part 3VLANs: one switch, several networks

Card C · What a VLAN is

A normal switch puts every port in one broadcast domain. A VLAN (virtual LAN) is a logical group of switch ports that behaves as its own separate LAN, with its own broadcast domain, regardless of where the devices physically are. Each VLAN has a number, the VLAN ID, and normally its own subnet. VLANs are set up in software on managed switches: no new hardware is needed.

  • Ports 1 to 12 in VLAN 10 (Staff) and ports 13 to 24 in VLAN 30 (Students): a broadcast sent from port 3 only reaches ports 1 to 12, even though it is one physical box.
  • A VLAN can span several switches on several floors. A finance PC on floor 2 and the payroll server on floor 3 can be in the same VLAN 20.
  • The link between two switches (a trunk) carries all the VLANs at once. Each frame on it carries a small VLAN tag that says which VLAN it belongs to, so the far switch delivers it only to ports in that VLAN.
  • Devices in different VLANs cannot talk directly, even on the same switch. Their traffic must go through a router or a Layer 3 switch (inter-VLAN routing), and that is exactly where firewall rules decide what is allowed: Students may reach the printer VLAN but not Finance.

Card D · Why VLANs, how they differ from subnets, and the costs

  • Security: sensitive devices are isolated; a compromised device in VLAN 30 cannot even see frames in VLAN 20; every crossing is a checkpoint.
  • Performance: broadcasts stay inside their VLAN, so each device receives far fewer of them.
  • Management: moving a user from Students to Staff is a port setting, not a new cable; groups are organized by role rather than by floor; a problem is contained to one VLAN.

Subnet vs VLAN in one line: subnetting divides the IP addresses and is enforced by routers at the internet layer; a VLAN divides the switches and is enforced by the switch at the network interface layer. Real networks use both: each VLAN gets its own subnet, and the router between them applies the rules.

The costs of segmenting. It adds complexity: every switch, router and firewall rule must be configured correctly, which needs skilled staff. A mistake can block legitimate traffic (a teacher who suddenly cannot reach the printer). And it needs ongoing maintenance: every new department or new type of device means updating the design.

Part 4Scenario: Westbridge Council offices

Westbridge Council is moving into a three-floor office building and has one IT technician. The building will hold:

  • 120 staff PCs spread over all three floors, plus 10 shared printers.
  • The finance team: 15 PCs on floor 2, a payroll server on floor 3, and one printer. Payroll data is confidential.
  • 40 IP CCTV cameras around the building, sending video all day to one recording server.
  • Public Wi-Fi in the ground-floor reception for visitors, up to 100 devices at a time.
  • Two public-facing servers (the council website and the online forms system) that people on the internet connect to.

All floors are cabled to managed switches that support VLANs, and the building has one router with a firewall.
